# Let the product, not the styling, carry the play

The best product sites of 2025–26 feel light because they spend very little visual weight, not because they are plain. A typical page has one short headline at weight 400–600 with tight tracking, one signature colour in a few large calm areas on white or warm off-white, and real product UI instead of decorative scenes. The playfulness sits in a single asset: a mascot, an inline chip or one illustration cluster. In Ordero's own category the bar is low and specific. The closest competitors (iKuchnia, Zamów Posiłek) win with literal order / pay / cancel messaging, the real parent screen in the hero, audience cards that name the caterer, a quick "I'm a parent – log in" exit and dated, sourced usage numbers. Lithuania's incumbent, e-maitinimas, still leads with stock canteen photography. Workplace-meal B2B sites almost all use food photography, the line "feed your team" and a demo button. That leaves the pickup mechanic, and the "no delivery fees, no subscriptions" promise already in Ordero Business's copy, unclaimed. The five concepts the client rejected used exactly what the research identifies as bulk: 800–900 display weights, ink outlines with hard offset shadows, a different saturated full-bleed colour in every section, and ornament everywhere. The five directions below are Live Month, Menu Board, Mint Rooms, Small Cast and Relay. Each keeps display type at 600 or lighter, allows at most one saturated area per screen, puts all the play in one place, and uses only typefaces whose Lithuanian and Polish glyphs were tested.

## Ordero's category still rewards literal verbs and real parent screens

Parent-facing products split into two headline registers. The modern childcare leaders use an outcome contrast: **"ECE is about joy, not operations"** ([Famly](https://www.famly.co/us)) and **"More time with children. Less time on admin."** ([Brightwheel](https://mybrightwheel.com)). Meal-ordering products in Central and Eastern Europe state the job plainly. iKuchnia's "Posiłki zamawiane i opłacane online – bez telefonów i ręcznych rozliczeń" ("meals ordered and paid online, no phone calls or manual settlements") names the verbs and the pain they remove ([iKuchnia](https://www.ikuchnia.com.pl/)). Zamów Posiłek runs an "order / pay / cancel" strip directly under its headline ([Zamów Posiłek](https://www.zamowposilek.pl/)). Ordero's fixed headline ("Mokyklos pietūs ar darželio maitinimas. Be rūpesčių per kelias minutes.", i.e. school lunches or kindergarten meals, hassle-free in a few minutes) already blends the two registers. Design therefore has to supply the concreteness the words leave out, and the strongest sites do that with real UI. iKuchnia's hero is an HTML parent panel showing the month, a struck-through cancelled day, "60,00 zł" due and a pickup QR code. Famly, Brightwheel, Kinderpedia and ELIIS also show real screens. **Heroes built only on stock photos of happy children (MySchoolBucks, Kinderly, Tadpoles, e-maitinimas) read as older** ([MySchoolBucks](https://www.myschoolbucks.com/ver2/getmain?requestAction=home); [e-maitinimas](https://e-maitinimas.lt/)).

Three-sided products handle their audiences in one of three ways:

- **A role picker in the hero.** Brightwheel offers "I'm an admin or director / I'm a staff member / I'm a parent or guardian" ([Brightwheel](https://mybrightwheel.com)); ClassDojo has five role tiles ([ClassDojo](https://www.classdojo.com)).
- **Audience cards or tabs that name the caterer.** Zamów Posiłek opens each of its four cards with a pain question; iKuchnia uses tabs plus a flow strip, "RODZIC → IKUCHNIA → KUCHNIA → WYDAWANIE" (parent → iKuchnia → kitchen → serving) ([iKuchnia](https://www.ikuchnia.com.pl/)).
- **A buyer-first site that sends parents to a login.** ParentPay does this with a top banner, Nutrislice with a utility bar ([ParentPay Group](https://www.parentpaygroup.com/); [Nutrislice](https://nutrislice.com/)).

**Caterers appear as a named audience only on the meal-specific sites**, which is why Ordero's platform section, with caterer, school and parents in one diagram, is a real asset to make prominent.

Proof in this category has two strands. The first is big numbers and review scores placed directly under the call-to-action. Brightwheel shows "4.9 | 160,000+ reviews" next to App Store and Capterra marks ([Brightwheel](https://mybrightwheel.com)). The second is dated, sourced operational counts. iKuchnia's stat bar ends with the footnote "Dane z systemu iKuchnia, październik 2026" ("data from the iKuchnia system, October 2026"). It also has a security block naming SSL, monitored OVH servers, twice-daily backups and its licensed payment operators ([iKuchnia](https://www.ikuchnia.com.pl/)). ClassDojo frames safety as a plain promise, "Privacy first—always" ([ClassDojo Privacy](https://www.classdojo.com/privacy-and-security/)), not as a wall of badges.

Ordero has a specific trust gap. **Its App Store listing carries a visible Lithuanian review complaining about commission charged to parents, and lists English as its only language** ([Ordero on App Store LT](https://apps.apple.com/lt/app/ordero/id6749176295)). On pricing, childcare software increasingly publishes per-child prices (ELIIS €1.50 per child per month, Storypark from $1.59) ([ELIIS](https://eliis.eu/en); [Storypark pricing](https://www.storypark.com/pricing)), while meal platforms are quote-only ([iKuchnia](https://www.ikuchnia.com.pl/)). On languages, ELIIS ships twelve site languages including LT and PL. iKuchnia advertises a PL/EN/UA parent panel, and e-maitinimas is Lithuanian-only ([ELIIS](https://eliis.eu/en); [e-maitinimas](https://e-maitinimas.lt/)). A clear LT/EN/PL switcher is therefore a small but real differentiator in Lithuania.

Visually, "modern" in this category means:

- **one grotesk family at 600–800 weight with negative tracking**, such as Famly's Matter at 96px/600/−4.8px;
- off-white or tinted backgrounds;
- a single saturated colour for calls-to-action;
- real UI fragments.

"Dated" means Lato or Roboto defaults, all-caps navigation, coloured H1s, square buttons, stock cut-outs and very long SEO pages ([Famly](https://www.famly.co/us); [Procare](https://www.procaresoftware.com/); [Kinderpedia](https://www.kinderpedia.co/en)). Even the modern local competitors run heavy: Zamów Posiłek sets 62px at weight 800 and iKuchnia uses Raleway 800. **A 500–600 display weight would set Ordero apart from its Polish peers as well as from the rejected concepts.**

## Workplace lunch sites sell to office managers with food photos

Almost every employer meal product leads with "feed your team" copy aimed at the office manager or HR buyer. Sharebite says "Feed your team wherever they work", Grubhub "Feed your team, fuel your business" and DoorDash "TEAM MEALS, HANDLED". **Only Forkable leads with a verb-first operations promise ("Automate Catering")** ([Sharebite](https://www.sharebite.com/); [DoorDash for Business](https://business.doordash.com/en-us); [Forkable](https://forkable.com/)). Hero visuals are overwhelmingly food or lifestyle photography, and real product UI is pushed below the fold. The big platforms pair a free self-serve sign-up with "contact sales".

Wolt for Work, Ordero Business's most direct competitor in Lithuania, splits sign-up by company size ("Under 50 employees… express sign up" versus a custom plan). It also uses local Vilnius testimonials, such as Wargaming's office manager ([Wolt for Work LT](https://corporate.wolt.com/pages/en/ltu/wolt-for-work)). Proof clusters around admin time saved: DoorDash shows "68+ HRS saved on meal coordination annually" next to quotes like "costing our teams 5H weekly". It also clusters around reliability, as with Forkable's "99.92% Accurate" ([DoorDash for Business](https://business.doordash.com/en-us); [Forkable](https://forkable.com/)).

Two openings matter for Ordero Business. First, **nobody owns the pickup moment visually**. Foodsby describes a central drop-off in its three steps, Sharebite describes "Stations", and only ezCater photographs shelves of labelled bags ([Foodsby](https://home.foodsby.com/); [ezCater](https://www.ezcater.com/)). Second, **"no delivery fees" as a structural claim is unowned**, because DoorDash and Uber attach $0 delivery to a subscription ([DoorDash for Business](https://business.doordash.com/en-us); [Uber for Business Eats](https://www.uber.com/us/en/business/solutions/eats/overview/)). Ordero Business's existing step-three copy, "Jokių pristatymo mokesčių, jokių prenumeratų, jokio laukimo" ("No delivery fees, no subscriptions, no waiting"), already makes that claim. It deserves typographic prominence, not body-text burial.

The modern end of the category looks one of two ways. Sharebite sets a **400-weight 66px headline on a warm white-to-beige ground, with a single yellow pill that has black text**. Bolt sets mint type on near-black green. The dated end uses Poppins, Roboto or Work Sans, uppercase 12px buttons with 3px radius, auto-rotating carousels and lead-form modals that fire on load ([Sharebite](https://www.sharebite.com/); [Bolt for Business](https://bolt.eu/en/business/); [Just Eat for Business](https://business.just-eat.co.uk/); [SmartLunch](https://smartlunch.com/pl/)).

## Best-in-class sites get lighter by cutting weight, colour count and ornament

Across family, fintech and SaaS leaders, display weight sorts into three camps:

| Camp | Typical display weight | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Refined SaaS and fintech | 400–510 | Cash App, Vercel, Granola (serif), Mercury, Linear |
| Taste-maker middle | 575–600 | Notion, Clay, Attio |
| Playful consumer and kids | 700–800 | Duolingo, Headspace, Monzo, Wise |

Almost all of them track display sizes tight, at −2% to −6% with line-height around 0.9–1.0 ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/); [Vercel](https://vercel.com/); [Granola](https://www.granola.ai/)). The kids' brands that go to 700 do it at modest sizes: **Duolingo's homepage H1 is only 32px/700 on a pure-white field**, and the play comes from its character cluster ([Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com/)).

Colour follows a "1 + 1 + n" rule:

- one signature hue used as a few large areas (Wise lime, Cash App green, GoHenry violet);
- one deep ink partner that is never pure black;
- pastels confined to cards and tags.

Warm off-whites in the #F4F1ED–#F7F7F2 range are the default "premium-friendly" ground ([Wise](https://wise.com/gb/); [Superhuman](https://superhuman.com/)). On layout, refined pages use **inset rounded panels on white rather than full-bleed slabs**. Monzo's hero photo sits in a 32px-radius container about 24px from the viewport edges. Pages are short (Notion's homepage is 4,731px, about five screens), and navigation has six items or fewer ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/); [Notion](https://www.notion.com/)).

On illustration, the documented movement is toward fewer characters:

- **Headspace's refresh set out to "uncomplicate our creative" by cutting literal characters** ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)).
- **Duolingo's February 2026 redesign removed header illustration that "can feel forced and take up space without conveying anything useful"**, moving to whitespace instead of containers and flatter cards ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/core-tabs-redesign/)).
- Notion keeps its line characters at the edges of the real product window ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/)).

Motion is ambient and product-led. Monzo runs 27 muted video loops of UI, Linear has 156 CSS-animated elements in a live HTML product mock, and Duolingo and Headspace use Lottie files only for character moments. No leader scroll-jacks ([Monzo](https://monzo.com/); [Linear](https://linear.app/); [Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com/)). Trust copy is short and absolute ("No ads. Ever.", "100% safe"), and seals appear as small badges ([Lingokids](https://lingokids.com/); [Monzo](https://monzo.com/)).

Commentators now flag several looks as dated:

- **The generic "AI slop" page: Inter everywhere, a purple gradient on white, a centred hero, three feature cards** ([Claude blog](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills); [925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells)).
- The samey dark "Linear look" ([LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design/)).
- Abstract 3D shapes and generic flat illustration ([SaaSFrame](https://www.saasframe.io/blog/10-saas-landing-page-trends-for-2026-with-real-examples)).

Neo-brutalism is contested. Some designers call it overused, while Figma still lists it as a trend ([SVGator](https://www.svgator.com/blog/what-is-neubrutalism/); [Figma](https://www.figma.com/resource-library/web-design-trends/)). No authoritative source declares claymorphism dated, so that judgement rests on the client's reaction, not the literature.

Practitioner guidance makes the client's word "bulky" concrete. Refactoring UI treats borders as a last resort and favours faint shadows and extra whitespace ([Refactoring UI notes](https://rjseymour66.github.io/webdev/refactoring-ui/)). UX Movement argues that nested borders add scanning noise ([UX Movement](https://uxmovement.substack.com/p/why-using-too-many-borders-hurts)). Studios building children's identities say the same thing. Little Troop said of MoMA Family Festival, "We couldn't go too wild or maximalist" ([The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/little-troop-brings-a-sense-of-childlike-wonder-to-its-playful-identity-for-the-moma-family-festival)). Gramática dropped "bright, loud colour schemes" for Wooly Organic in favour of "Simplicity in all brand elements" ([The Brand Identity](https://the-brandidentity.com/project/gram%C3%A1tica-studio-strips-back-childrens-brand-clich%C3%A9s-in-its-refined-identity-for-wooly-organic)).

Mapped onto the rejected work, bulk is the sum of:

- 800–900 display weight carried below the hero;
- 2–3px outlines plus hard offset shadows;
- a different saturated full-bleed field in every section;
- three brand colours at equal strength;
- large radii on every container;
- an ornament (sticker, tilt, clay, handwriting) in every section.

The light alternative takes one step down on each of these. No empirical study links these properties to perceived bulk; this is practitioner consensus.

## Fixed copy, three audiences and long Lithuanian lines set the constraints

Every direction below works within the same constraints.

**Headline length.** The LT and PL hero first lines run 38–39 characters ("Mokyklos pietūs ar darželio maitinimas.", "Obiady szkolne i posiłki przedszkolne."). The longest H2 is the Polish CTA line at 55 characters (`src/i18n/content.ts`). As a rule of thumb (my estimate, to be checked in the browser), a 39-character line at about 0.5em average glyph width needs roughly 20em. That means either a full-width headline at about 56–64px at 1440px, or about 36–40px in a half-width column. **Split heroes with a big headline beside a big visual will force LT and PL to wrap to three or more lines**, so most directions stack the headline above the product.

**Typefaces.** Every face named below passed a glyph test for all 16 LT/PL lowercase letters and their capitals. Fredoka and Urbanist fail and are excluded. The research team ran this test itself on font files from [Google Fonts](https://fonts.google.com/metadata/fonts) and Fontshare. The directions deliberately avoid the faces the rejected concepts used:

- Bricolage Grotesque at 800;
- Poppins at 700;
- Pilcrow Rounded;
- Chillax with Caveat;
- Nunito at 900.

Poppins also ranks 8th in Google Fonts popularity, so it reads as default. **Nunito Sans, the design system's text face, was not in the tested set.** Run the same glyph check before using it anywhere outside the app UI fragments.

**Colour contrast** (my calculations from the hex values):

| Pairing | Ratio | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Ink #18181B on mint #3ECFA3 | about 9.0:1 | Passes for text |
| Ink on gold #FFBE00 | about 10.6:1 | Passes for text |
| Ink on coral #FF4D5E | about 5.5:1 | Passes for text |
| White on mint | about 2.0:1 | Fails |
| White on gold | about 1.7:1 | Fails |
| White on coral | about 3.2:1 | Large text only |

**Mint, gold and coral therefore carry ink labels, and never act as text colours on white.** Contrast is a common failure: 83.9% of top home pages have sub-AA text ([Involve.me citing WebAIM](https://www.involve.me/blog/landing-page-trends)).

**The project's build skill.** `.claude/skills/design-taste-frontend/SKILL.md` bans eyebrows, gradients, shadows on resting elements, hairline rules, monospace, glass and floating pill navigation, styled accent words in headlines, icon-topped card rows, and more than two typefaces. That rules out some devices the research praises: Notion's inline pill word, Granola's dashed hairline grid, Lovevery's italic accent word and Headspace's gradient. The directions below are written to pass these rules. Real product fragments marked `data-ui` keep the app's own borders, Poppins and Nunito Sans, and radii of 14/18px.

**Positioning moves the fixed copy already allows:**

- Keep the partners line ("Pasitiki daugiau nei 100 partnerių", "trusted by more than 100 partners") inside the first viewport, as Notion does with its logo strip.
- Let the nav's existing "Užsakyti demo" / "Prisijungti" pair act as the school-and-caterer versus parent fork.
- Treat the platform section as the three-audience split.
- Set the four stats as one quiet row, as Greenlight does ([Greenlight](https://greenlight.com/)).
- Use the testimonials as they stand: they already name a teacher, a caterer and a mother of two, the same role-naming iKuchnia uses.

Raise three content gaps with the client separately: a plain statement of any cost to parents, a short security block, and dated, sourced counts.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Live Month | The parent's real month of meals is the hero | Instrument Sans 600 / 400 | Mint as state only | Full-width headline over a live HTML panel |

### 4. Small Cast: white page, illustrations as the only play

| Spec | Detail |
|---|---|
| Idea | As on Duolingo, all the playfulness lives in Ordero's existing spot illustrations, shown large, one per section, on a quiet white page with modest type. |

## Conclusion

The research turns "bulky and not nice" from taste into something measurable: display weight above 600, outlines and hard shadows, the number of saturated colour fields per screen, and the number of ornaments per section. All five directions sit at or under the thresholds the refined leaders use. Each spends the playfulness budget in exactly one place: the product (Live Month), the menu sheet (Menu Board), the mint rooms (Mint Rooms), the characters (Small Cast) or the loop (Relay). That single-asset rule, more than any palette or font choice, separates current children's and family brands from the 2022–24 sticker-and-outline default.

If the client wants the lowest-risk pick, Live Month and Mint Rooms track the evidence most closely. Menu Board stands out most against direct competitors, because no school or workplace meal-ordering site in the notes sets its headlines in a serif. Relay best carries the three-sided story that only Ordero, iKuchnia and Zamów Posiłek can tell. Two caveats apply. All observations are desktop captures at 1440px taken during Black Friday week, with no mobile layouts. And the LT/PL line-length and contrast figures are my calculations, so verify them in the browser before committing.
